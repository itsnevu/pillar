How Pyris Pact Turns a Work Agreement Into a Self-Settling Contract on Arc and Robinhood Chain

Cross-border B2B payments are one of the largest flows of money on earth and one of the slowest. Industry estimates put the annual volume of cross-border B2B transactions above 150 trillion dollars, yet the dominant rail underneath them, the correspondent-banking wire, still takes one to five business days to clear, charges 25 to 50 dollars per leg, and loses further value to FX spreads that routinely exceed one percent. For a global agency paying a contractor in another jurisdiction, the money is effectively frozen for most of a working week while both parties simply wait.

The freelance economy sits on top of the same problem and adds a second tax. Upwork moved to a flat 10 percent service fee for freelancers in 2023, Fiverr takes 20 percent of every order, and dedicated escrow providers such as Escrow.com charge a tiered fee of roughly 0.9 to 3.25 percent on top of the underlying bank transfer. On a 10,000 dollar engineering engagement, the person who did the work can lose anywhere from several hundred to two thousand dollars before the funds reach an account they control. The platform's value is trust, and trust has been priced like a monopoly.

Pyris Pact is a programmable B2B payments protocol that replaces the intermediary with a deterministic state machine. A client locks a dollar stablecoin into a milestone escrow before work begins, the contractor submits proof of delivery onchain, and the client releases the funds with a single transaction. The contract, PyrisPact.sol, is one Solidity file with no owner, no admin key, no pause switch, no upgrade proxy, and no fee parameter. It exposes nine state-changing functions, createPact, extendDeadline, submitWork, releaseFunds, refund, dispute, proposeResolution, arbitrate, and withdraw, along with read functions such as getPact, getClientPacts, getVendorPacts, and getPacts for indexing. The full function reference and escrow flow are documented at pyris.tech/docs.

Pyris Pact is dual chain. The same contract is deployed on two networks that solve the stablecoin settlement problem from opposite directions. On Arc, Circle's purpose-built Layer 1 for stablecoin finance, chain ID 5042, PyrisPact lives at 0xb5f905f48321F44e379d8680e947dDd05830AF62. On Robinhood Chain, an Arbitrum Orbit Layer 2 operated by Robinhood, chain ID 4663, the same source is deployed against USDG, the Global Dollar issued by Paxos. A Pact lives on exactly one of them. Pact IDs, events, and balances are per deployment, so the dashboard always shows a single selected network and every read and write is addressed to it explicitly. Nothing bridges, nothing is mirrored, and no pact ever depends on the state of the other chain.

The two deployments differ in exactly one thing, fixed at deploy time: how the escrowed dollars enter the contract. Arc's defining property is that USDC is the native gas token, exposed by the EVM with 18 decimals. PyrisPact is deployed there in native mode, meaning the escrow amount is passed as transaction value rather than through an ERC-20 approve-and-transfer sequence. A client locking 1,000 USDC pays a sub-cent network fee denominated in the same asset and never has to acquire ETH or any other volatile token to interact with the protocol. On Robinhood Chain gas is paid in ETH, so PyrisPact is deployed in ERC-20 mode: the client approves the contract for the milestone amount, and createPact pulls USDG with a transferFrom, in the token's own six decimals. Two transactions instead of one on the first step, and a small amount of ETH in the wallet, in exchange for settling on a network whose users already hold their dollars as USDG.

Everything after funding is identical on both chains, because it is the same bytecode reading nothing but its own storage and the block timestamp. Each Pact moves through six states, encoded onchain as FUNDED, SUBMITTED, RELEASED, REFUNDED, DISPUTED, and RESOLVED. A Pact is born FUNDED the moment the client's stablecoin is locked, which is the moment the contractor can verify the deposit directly on the Arc explorer or on the Robinhood Chain Blockscout. It transitions to SUBMITTED when the contractor attaches a submission note, typically a pull request URL, a repository link, or a design file reference; a submission after the deadline is rejected, so a contractor cannot race a refund. It terminates in RELEASED when the client approves, in REFUNDED when the deadline passes without delivery or the contractor voluntarily cancels, and it can be flagged DISPUTED by either party when the deliverable does not match the agreed scope. A disputed Pact freezes and ends in RESOLVED only when both parties propose the same split, in basis points, or when an arbiter the client named at creation records a ruling.

Settlement speed is the second structural difference from the bank rail, and both chains deliver it. Arc produces blocks with sub-second finality; Robinhood Chain confirms in seconds as an Arbitrum-based rollup. On either network the interval between a client calling releaseFunds and the contractor holding spendable dollars is measured in seconds rather than days. Compared with a SWIFT wire, that is a reduction in settlement latency of roughly five orders of magnitude. Compared with a platform payout, which typically adds its own five-to-fourteen-day holding period before the bank transfer even starts, the difference is larger still.

Protection in the protocol runs in both directions, which is what distinguishes escrow from a simple prepayment. The contractor is protected because the funds are provably locked and cannot be withdrawn unilaterally by the client while the Pact is FUNDED or SUBMITTED. The client is protected because the deadline is enforced by the contract itself, and once it lapses without a submission the full amount can be reclaimed. Neither party depends on the goodwill of the other, and neither depends on the developers of Pyris Pact, who hold no custodial role and no ability to move deposited funds on either chain.

It is equally important to state what the protocol does not do. A smart contract can verify that a submission occurred and that a deadline was met, but no EVM opcode can judge whether a brand identity is visually strong or whether a Next.js frontend is sufficiently responsive. Qualitative review remains a human decision made offchain. Pyris Pact is therefore designed around explicit submissions, client-side approval, and a DISPUTED status that acts as a flag for offchain negotiation rather than an onchain arbitrator. Without a named arbiter, a dispute both parties refuse to settle leaves the funds locked, and that is the honest limit of a two-party contract. The protocol automates the payment rail, not the judgment. The whitepaper at pyris.tech/whitepaper sets out these boundaries in detail, together with the trust model and the four design pillars behind the contract.

For the operator, the frontend at pyris.tech surfaces this state machine as a working dashboard. The dashboard at pyris.tech/app aggregates total escrow volume, active and settled counts, and filters Pacts by outgoing (as client) and incoming (as contractor) on the selected network, while the public directory shows every registered Pact with its amount, status, deadline, and counterparties. A network switch in the shell moves between Arc and Robinhood Chain; before every write the app asks the wallet to switch to that chain, so a transaction is never sent to the wrong network by accident. The application is built on Next.js with wagmi and viem, connects through any injected wallet, and can be pointed at a local Anvil chain or either mainnet through environment variables alone.

The broader thesis is that stablecoins have reached the scale where B2B settlement no longer needs to route through banks at all, and that the settlement layer should be chosen by where the dollars already sit, not by where a protocol happens to be deployed. USDC circulation has grown into the tens of billions of dollars, and Arc exists specifically to give that liquidity a settlement layer with predictable fees and instant finality. Robinhood Chain brings a second pool of onchain dollars, held as USDG by a retail brokerage's users, onto the same escrow logic. Pyris Pact is a narrow, honest application of both. It does not promise to replace contracts, courts, or client relationships. It promises that when a client says the work is done, the money moves in the same second, in full, and in the currency both parties already agreed on.
