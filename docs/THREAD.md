1/
You sold it. It doubled four months later.

Everyone reading this has done it once, and remembers the exact number.

Selling the thing you were right about is the most expensive mistake in investing.

Pillar Finance exists so you never have to. 🧵

2/
The problem is not that you were wrong.

You needed cash. Rent, a bill, an opportunity, anything.

Your portfolio was the only place it could come from, so you closed a position you believed in to fund a life you were already living.

3/
There is a version of this that rich people never do.

They do not sell. They borrow against what they own, and keep the upside.

That option has always existed. It has just never been available to you.

4/
Pillar: deposit tokenized stocks as collateral, borrow USDG against them.

Your position stays open. Your exposure stays intact. You just have cash now.

Nothing was sold.

5/
But a normal loan gives you a new problem: a repayment schedule.

So Pillar removes that too.

Your collateral does not sit idle while it backs the loan. It earns. And that yield is routed straight to your debt.

6/
The loan repays itself.

No monthly payment. No due date. No refinancing.

Every harvest moves what your collateral earned onto the debt. Anyone can trigger one, including you. Time pays it down, not you.

7/
Say it plainly:

Deposit $10k of stock tokens.
Borrow $4k USDG.
Collateral keeps earning.
That yield eats the $4k over time.
Debt hits zero. Collateral comes back whole.

You never sold. You never repaid.

8/
How the machine is wired:

Collateral in → routed to yield vaults → yield accrues → harvest applies it straight to your debt.

Not a rewards page you claim for yourself. A debt number that only ever goes down.

9/
The honest version of that: if yield goes to zero, your debt stops shrinking.

It never grows on its own, but it does not vanish either.

Anyone describing a self-repaying loan without saying that is selling you a brochure.

10/
Now the hard part, and it is specific to stocks.

Equity markets close. Your debt does not.

A stock can gap 15% overnight against collateral backing a loan that has been live the entire time nobody could trade it.

11/
That is why LTV here is deliberately boring.

30–50% depending on the market. Tighter for volatile single names, widest for a broad ETF basket.

The right answer to overnight gap risk is not a clever mechanism. It is not lending you as much.

12/
Pricing is oracle-driven with staleness rules.

If a collateral asset has no fresh, defensible price, Pillar does not guess. New borrows against it pause.

Refusing to quote beats liquidating someone on a number nobody can defend.

13/
Liquidation exists. Pretending otherwise would be a lie.

But it is partial, not total: it sells the smallest slice that restores your ratio, never the whole position.

And your distance to it is on the dashboard before it ever becomes urgent.

14/
What the dashboard actually shows:

Live LTV. Remaining debt. Yield rate flowing to it. Estimated time to zero. Distance to liquidation, in percent, updated continuously.

You should never be surprised by your own position.

One loop. Each piece makes the others worth more.
